===== Research regarding Steam player base by language for automation and incremental games =====

While Valve does not publicly release exact language percentages broken down by genre,
comprehensive data from indie game publishers and localized sales for automation (e.g., Factorio, Satisfactory) and incremental/idle games (e.g., Cookie Clicker) reveal a distribution that looks remarkably different from the standard Steam platform average.
Automation and incremental games are heavily driven by text, complex systems, and community-made wiki guides.
Because of this, their demographics skew heavily toward western and specific highly technical Asian player bases.

Why the Metrics Shift for These Genres
The "German Simulation" Phenom: The joke that "Germans play job simulators after work" holds true in data. German representation routinely doubles or triples its baseline Steam average when analyzing sales numbers for deep automation loops.
The Wiki Dependency: Incremental and automation games are notoriously reliant on external documentation, calculators, and community forums. Because these secondary communities (like Discord and Reddit) operate predominantly in English, the genre naturally filters for players comfortable with English interfaces.
Localization Impact: For small, experimental indie titles in these genres, releasing only in English covers roughly 60% of the target market. Adding German, Chinese, and Japanese localized text generally captures up to 85% of the total addressable audience for optimization games.

Prioritizing English, Simplified Chinese, German, and Japanese offers the highest return on investment (ROI) for an indie automation or incremental title.
Because automation and incremental mechanics rely heavily on text, math, and UI tooltips, localization directly dictates whether players can engage with your core loop.
Tiered Localization Priority

Tier 1: Essential (Day-One Support)
English: Serves as the global baseline, covering native English speakers and a massive chunk of Northern/Western Europe.
Simplified Chinese: Chinese gamers rarely purchase games without Chinese text, even if they have some English literacy. Adding Chinese immediately expands your total addressable market.
German: Germany consistently represents the highest-spending, most engaged demographic outside the US for simulation, management, and automation games.
Japanese: Incremental, idle, and clicker mechanics perform well in Japan, where players appreciate intricate number scaling and mobile/desktop idle formats.

Tier 2: Strong Secondary Returns
Russian: Russian players form a large portion of Steam's active user base. Purchasing power per unit is lower due to regional pricing, but wishlist volume and community mods can boost overall discoverability.
French: Solid regional preference for deep strategy and management systems.Korean: Highly engaged with grind-heavy, mathematical progression mechanics.

Tier 3: Expand Post-Launch
Spanish (Spain & Latin America) & Brazilian Portuguese: High player volume on Steam overall, but slightly lower engagement with automation/incremental niches compared to action or survival titles. Best considered after validating your core sales.

Recommended Localization StrategyLocalize the Steam Page First: Before translating the entire game, localize your Steam store page and demo into Simplified Chinese, German, and Japanese to test wishlist conversion rates across regions.

Format for UI Expansion: German words are notoriously longer than English equivalents, while CJK (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) characters require specific font sizing and line heights. Ensure UI text boxes can dynamically resize or wrap cleanly.

Community Crowdsourcing: Automation and incremental communities love contributing. Games like Factorio and RimWorld leveraged community translation platforms (such as Crowdin or Weblate) to support dozens of secondary languages with minimal upfront development cost.
